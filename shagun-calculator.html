The shagun ladder

Shagun amounts tend to move in familiar steps: ₹101, ₹251, ₹501, ₹1,100, ₹2,100, ₹3,100, ₹5,100, ₹7,100, ₹11,000, ₹21,000, ₹31,000, ₹51,000. Pick the step that matches how close you are, then move up one if you’re going as a family or the function is lavish.

Questions

Why does shagun end in 1, like ₹501 or ₹1,100?

In many Indian families an amount ending in zero is seen as an ending, while the extra one stands for a new beginning and blessings that keep growing. So ₹500 becomes ₹501, and ₹1,000 is usually rounded up to ₹1,100 or given as ₹1,001.

How much shagun should I give at a wedding?

It depends on how close you are, how many of you are attending and how grand the function is. As a rough guide for a banquet wedding in a big city, colleagues often give around ₹1,100, friends ₹2,100 to ₹3,100, and close family ₹5,100 or more. Use the calculator above for your situation.

Should I give shagun if I’m already giving a gift?

Not necessarily. A gift on its own is fine. Some families add a small shagun, like ₹101 or ₹501, as a token of blessing alongside the gift.

Can I give shagun online by UPI?

Yes, and it’s increasingly common. If the hosts made a Bash with a shagun fund, you can pick an amount, add your blessings and pay from your UPI app in seconds.

Is this calculator an exact rule?

No. It’s a starting point based on common practice. Your budget, your family’s customs and what the hosts mean to you matter more than any number.
