A wedding gift registry that works for Indian weddings

Most couples end up with four pressure cookers and a stack of envelopes. Put the gifts you actually need on one list, add a shagun fund for everyone who prefers cash, and send it with the invite.

Gifts and shagun in one link

Friends can pick the air fryer; your mama ji can send ₹5,100 by UPI. Both land on the same page, and you see who gave what.

No doubles, no awkward returns

When someone reserves a gift it’s marked taken for everyone else. Nobody has to coordinate in the family group.

Nothing to install

Guests open the link and go. No account, no app, which matters when half your guest list is over 60.

How to share your registry without it feeling awkward

  • Send the link a day or two after the invite, not inside it. A short line works: “A few of you asked what we need, so we made a list.”
  • Let parents and siblings forward it. It reads as helpful when it comes from family rather than the couple.
  • Keep a range of prices. If everything costs ₹20,000, people quietly skip the list and give an envelope anyway.
  • Turn on the shagun fund. Many guests will give cash no matter what; this way it reaches your UPI with their blessings attached instead of getting lost in envelopes.

Questions

Is it rude to have a wedding registry in India?

It’s becoming normal, especially among friends and younger relatives who would rather give something useful. Pairing the list with a shagun option keeps it comfortable for everyone, because guests who prefer tradition can still give cash.

How many gifts should we put on our wedding registry?

Roughly one gift for every three to four guest households, across a spread of prices. You can add more as gifts get reserved.

Can guests give shagun online instead of an envelope?

Yes. Turn on the shagun fund and guests pick an amount like ₹1,100 or ₹5,100, add their blessings, and pay straight to your UPI ID. Bash never touches the money.
